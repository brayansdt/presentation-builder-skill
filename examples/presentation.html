AI Development at Scale

How engineering teams move faster without losing architectural control

AI amplifies what you already have — good habits and bad ones

  • A team with clear conventions ships faster with AI. A team without ships chaos, faster.
  • Treating AI as a productivity layer on top of an undisciplined process is the core mistake
  • Engineering culture does not become less important with AI — it becomes more consequential
  • The output ceiling is still engineering judgment, even when the floor is much higher

Architectural discipline required

Generating code is solved. Keeping your architecture coherent is not.

  • Any capable model writes a working endpoint in seconds — that is table stakes now
  • The hard problem is cross-repo consistency, naming conventions, and shared abstractions
  • Inconsistent architecture is invisible until it is not — and then it is a rewrite
  • Multi-repo organizations have compounding drift risk that single-repo teams do not

In a multi-repo org, AI has no memory of the decisions you made last quarter

  • Context is per-session — the AI does not remember the ADR you wrote in February
  • Without explicit context, AI defaults to common patterns, not your patterns
  • Each repo becomes a slightly different interpretation of the same intent
  • What starts as a style difference becomes an integration failure at the boundary
Repo A — Auth service
Decision made: JWT with short-lived tokens, refresh pattern documented in ADR-14
Repo B — 3 months later
AI generates session-based auth. Looks fine. Nobody flags it. Shipped.
Integration — 6 months later
Two incompatible auth systems. Rewrite required. Origin: a prompt with no context.

Most teams adopted AI tools before they adopted AI conventions

  • Ad-hoc prompting produces ad-hoc output — it just produces it faster
  • Without shared prompts, every engineer makes different assumptions about what "good" looks like
  • Without guardrails, AI helps junior engineers move fast in the wrong direction
  • The cost of inconsistency compounds across every PR, every dependency, every deploy
Speed without structure produces volume, not quality. AI-generated drift is the new technical debt — and it accumulates faster.

Architectural control comes from codifying what your team already knows

01 · Shared Prompts
Reusable prompt templates
Team-approved prompts that embed your conventions, your stack, and your non-obvious decisions. Reduces drift at the source before the first line is generated.
02 · Decision Records
Architecture Decision Records (ADRs)
Structured docs that give AI the context it does not have by default — why you chose Postgres over MongoDB, why you avoided GraphQL here, what the non-obvious constraint is.
03 · Code Examples
Canonical reference implementations
Approved examples AI can reference and replicate. Not what the model thinks is typical — what your team has decided is correct for this system.
04 · Review Guardrails
Explicit AI-PR review criteria
Clear, written criteria for what AI-generated code must pass before merge: scope boundaries, dependency rules, test coverage floors, architectural boundary checks.

AI-generated code demands higher review discipline, not lower

  • Reviewers skip reading what looks clean — AI code always looks clean
  • The logic errors, architectural violations, and missing edge cases are still there
  • Define explicit criteria for AI PRs: scope, dependencies, test coverage
  • Raise the bar hardest at the architectural boundary: shared libs, public APIs, cross-service contracts

Every team can build three governance artifacts in one sprint

  • Conventions doc: your patterns, your non-obvious decisions, and the reasoning behind them
  • Prompt library: 5–10 tested, team-approved prompts for your most common tasks
  • Review checklist: explicit pass criteria for architecture, scope, and coverage on AI PRs
  • A working draft shipped this sprint is worth more than a perfect policy written next quarter
Day 1–2 · Conventions
Write it from memory. Where the team disagrees is where you need clarity most.
Day 3–4 · Prompt Library
Build from your most common AI tasks. Test each prompt on a real task before approving it.
Day 5 · Review Checklist
Draft it, then ship it with your next AI-generated PR. It will improve immediately.

A three-phase path from ad-hoc AI to governed AI development

  • Audit: map where AI is used today and identify the patterns of drift
  • Pilot: build the four artifacts and run one team on the new conventions for 4–6 weeks
  • Scale: propagate conventions, instrument for compliance, iterate on what breaks
  • Measure architecture coherence across repos — not PR velocity or line count
Phase 1 · Audit
Map AI usage across repos. Surface undocumented decisions and consistency gaps.
Phase 2 · Build & Pilot
Create the four artifacts. Run one pilot team for 4–6 weeks. Measure what improves.
Phase 3 · Scale
Propagate to all teams. Add tooling for convention compliance. Revisit quarterly.

The bottom line

The goal is not to replace engineers. It is to raise the quality and speed of execution — without outsourcing the architectural judgment that makes it last.
